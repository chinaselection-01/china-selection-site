How to verify a Chinese factory is real

Before you transfer a deposit, you want one answer: is the company on the other end a real manufacturer with real production — or a middleman, or worse, a fictional listing? Here is a practical order of checks, cheapest first.

1. Confirm the legal entity exists

Every registered Chinese company appears in the National Enterprise Credit Information Publicity System (gsxt.gov.cn), a government-run registry. Search by the exact Chinese company name. You should see a unified social credit code, registration status (存续 = active), legal representative, and registered capital. A supplier who cannot give you the exact registered name is a red flag.

2. Separate manufacturer from trader

Both are legitimate businesses. The problem is being told you are buying from a factory when you are not. Tell-tale signs of a trading company:

  • They list products from unrelated categories with no shared production logic.
  • They are reluctant to show the workshop or live video of your specific product being made.
  • Their address is a commercial office, not an industrial zone.

Ask directly: “Is this produced in your own facility?” A real manufacturer will show you the line. A trader will pivot.

3. Watch for hidden subcontracting

A factory can accept your order and quietly outsource it to a cheaper workshop you never approved. The result: goods that do not match the sealed sample. Mitigate by stating in the contract that the named facility is the production site, and by booking a pre-shipment inspection (AQL 2.5 is the common standard).

4. Verify export records, not just a catalogue

A catalogue proves they can describe a product. Export records prove they have actually shipped it. On China Selection, every product page states the HS code and declaration elements taken from the factory’s actual export documentation — so you can see the product has cleared customs before, not just that it exists in a photo.

5. For high-value orders, inspect or visit

Above a certain amount, a third-party inspection (SGS, BV, Intertek, or a local auditor) is cheaper than a bad shipment. A short on-site audit confirms capacity, working conditions, and that the workshop matches the claim. For most small buyers this is optional; for repeat or high-volume orders it is standard practice.

Frequently asked questions

Can I verify a Chinese company online without visiting?
Partially. The national registry (gsxt.gov.cn) confirms the entity exists, and export records show prior shipments. But confirming live production still needs a visit or third-party inspection.
What is the difference between a manufacturer and a trading company?
A manufacturer owns or controls production and can show the workshop and in-process goods. A trader sources and resells. Both are legitimate; the risk is being misled about which one you are dealing with.
Why does hidden subcontracting matter?
Outsourced production may differ from the approved sample and bypass your quality checks. State the production facility in the contract and book pre-shipment inspection to limit the risk.
