How much does a China sourcing agent cost in 2026

The headline number — “5% commission” — is the least useful part of the cost. What matters is what is bundled in, and what is earned on the side.

Typical commission bands

For agents who charge an explicit fee, transparent quotes generally land in a 3–10% of order value range:

Large repeat orders
~3–5% — volume and familiarity reduce hands-on work.
Mid-size orders
~5–8% — typical for ongoing relationships.
Small first-time orders
~8–10% — supplier search, sampling, and inspection take real time.

Below roughly 1–2%, it is difficult to sustain genuine verification and inspection work — which is why very low headline rates usually mean the agent earns elsewhere.

How “free” agents actually earn

Agents who quote “no commission” are rarely working for nothing. A common model is a supplier rebate built into the unit price: the factory pays the agent a percentage, and the buyer pays a slightly higher unit cost without seeing a line item. This is not automatically wrong — many legitimate businesses run on it — but it becomes a problem when it is hidden and not disclosed.

The practical difference for you: with an explicit commission, you can compare the agent’s fee against the service. With a hidden rebate, you cannot tell whether you are overpaying on the product itself.

How to read a transparent quote

  • A clear split between product cost and service fee.
  • The HS code and declaration elements stated, so you can check your own duty.
  • Packing data (cartons, gross weight, volume) included for freight planning.
  • Any supplier rebate disclosed in writing.

If a quote cannot show these, the true cost is unclear regardless of the headline percentage. On China Selection, product pages state the HS code and declaration elements from the factory’s actual export records so the customs side of the cost is visible before you negotiate.

Frequently asked questions

What commission do China sourcing agents charge?
Transparent agents commonly quote 3–10% of order value: lower bands for large repeat orders, higher for small first-time orders. Below ~1–2% it is hard to sustain real verification work.
How do “free” sourcing agents make money?
Often through a supplier rebate built into the unit price rather than an explicit fee. The buyer sees “no commission” but may pay a higher unit cost. The issue is when the rebate is hidden.
How do I read a transparent quote?
Look for a clear split between product cost and service fee, the HS code and declaration elements stated, packing data included, and any supplier rebate disclosed.
