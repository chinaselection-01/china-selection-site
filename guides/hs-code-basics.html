HS codes & import duty

The single number that decides whether your shipment clears customs cheaply or gets stuck at the port is the HS code. Understanding it is the cheapest risk control in importing.

What an HS code actually is

The Harmonized System (HS) code is a standardized numerical code used by customs worldwide to classify traded products. It has at least six digits: the first two identify the chapter (product group), the next two the heading, the next two the subheading. Most countries then add 2–4 more digits for their own tariff lines.

Example from our own listings: cat trees and similar textile pet furniture fall under HS 6307900090 — “other made-up articles” — in the 6307 heading. Wooden chicken coops we list under HS 4421999090 — “other articles of wood.” Same category family, completely different duty treatment.

Why the same product can face different duty

Two importers can buy an identical product and pay different tariffs, because:

  • The destination country sets its own rates on the same six-digit base.
  • Trade measures (anti-dumping duties, Section 301 tariffs, FTAs) change the effective rate regardless of the base HS code.
  • Material and purpose wording in the declaration can move the product between subheadings.

This is why a supplier saying “don’t worry about customs, we handle it” is not reassuring — the buyer is the importer of record and bears the duty and the risk.

Declaration elements: the detail behind the number

The HS code is only half of it. Customs also wants declaration elements: material composition, intended use, brand, model. For a felt cat tree that means “100% polyester plush + paper-tube frame, for pet use, unbranded or private-label.” Get the elements wrong and the code is meaningless.

Frequently asked questions

Who decides the HS code at import?
The importer (or their broker) declares it to their own customs authority. The same product may be classified differently by different countries within the first six digits, so your duty depends on your destination.
If the factory gives me an HS code, is that the final duty?
It is the starting point. Your country may classify it under a different subheading with a different rate. Confirm the rate with your own broker before a volume order.
Why does China Selection show the HS code on every listing?
Because the HS code and declaration elements determine whether goods clear and at what cost. Showing them up front lets a buyer check duty before negotiating.
