China vs. other sourcing origins

“Source from China” is not a universal answer. The right origin depends on your product, your market’s tariffs, and how fast you need to move. Here is a straight comparison without the usual bias.

What actually differs by origin

Speed to sample
China’s supplier density usually means faster prototyping and shorter iteration loops for standardized goods.
MOQ flexibility
Large Chinese clusters compete on volume but many small workshops still accept lower MOQs than consolidated overseas factories.
Landed cost
Unit price is only part of it. Duty, freight, and trade measures decide the total. An origin with a free-trade agreement with your market can win even at a higher unit price.
Material & craft fit
Some products are tied to local inputs or skills (certain textiles, specific woods). Forcing them to China can add cost and risk.

A note on India and other “alternative” origins

It is sometimes assumed that other manufacturing origins lack the ability to export to Western markets. In practice, major suppliers in places like India have long exported to the EU and US and hold recognised certifications (OEKO-TEX, GOTS, BSCI and similar). The real question is not “can they export” but “what is my total landed cost and lead time versus the alternative.” For standardized goods, China typically remains the fastest to prototype and scale; for products with local material or FTA advantages, another origin can be cheaper at the border.

Why the HS code still matters more than the flag

The HS code describes the product, not where it was made. But the duty you pay depends on both the code and the trade relationship between the origin country and your market. So the same cat tree can land cheaper from one origin than another purely because of tariff treatment — even if the unit price was identical. Frequently asked questions

Is China still the cheapest place to manufacture?
Not always on unit price. Labour gaps have narrowed and some origins have FTAs that cut your import duty. China’s edge is usually scale, supply-chain density, and speed-to-sample.
When does a non-China origin make sense?
When an FTA between the origin and your market lowers duty, or when a product is tied to local materials or craft. For most standardized goods, China remains fastest to prototype and scale.
Does origin change the HS code?
No. The HS code describes the product. But the duty depends on both the code and the trade relationship between origin and your market.
