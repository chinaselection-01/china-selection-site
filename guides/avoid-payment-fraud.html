How to avoid payment fraud when sourcing from China

Most sourcing losses are not “theft” in the dramatic sense. They are deposits sent to a supplier who was never who they claimed to be. A few habits remove the bulk of that risk.

The payment structure that most buyers use

For a first order with a new supplier, the widely used structure is:

  • Deposit (commonly ~30%) via Telegraphic Transfer (T/T) before production begins.
  • Balance paid against the bill of lading (B/L) copy after production and inspection — meaning the goods exist and are booked before you release the rest.

This keeps the supplier motivated to ship (they have not been paid in full) while limiting your exposure (you have not paid in full before seeing proof of shipment). It is not fraud-proof, but it is the industry standard for good reason.

Why a platform should not hold your money

It sounds reassuring when a platform says “we hold your payment until you confirm receipt.” In practice, a platform that pools buyer funds into its own account becomes a target for disputes and regulatory scrutiny, and your money can be frozen if a separate transaction is contested. A cleaner model: payments go through a licensed payment institution (a regulated entity with segregated accounts), and the platform stays out of the money flow. You keep control; the platform keeps its role as a connector, not a custodian.

On China Selection, payments are handled by licensed payment institutions. We do not hold buyer funds and we do not provide payment guarantees. The exact route is agreed between buyer and factory — see How Buying Works.

Red flags worth pausing for

  • A request for 100% prepayment to a personal account on a first order.
  • Refusal to share a verifiable, registered company name you can check in the national registry.
  • Prices far below the market with no explanation (overcapacity, seconds, or a scam).
  • Pressure to move the conversation off the platform immediately.

None of these is proof on its own. Together, they justify slowing down and verifying — see our guide on verifying a Chinese factory.

Frequently asked questions

What is a safe payment structure for a first order?
A deposit (often ~30%) by T/T before production, with the balance paid against the bill of lading copy after production and inspection. Avoid 100% prepayment to an unverified supplier.
Should a sourcing platform hold my money?
Not necessarily. A platform pooling buyer funds becomes a dispute and regulatory target. A transparent model routes payments through a licensed payment institution and keeps the platform out of the money flow.
What are the most common red flags?
100% prepayment to a personal account, refusal to share a verifiable company name, prices far below market, and pressure to leave the official platform.
